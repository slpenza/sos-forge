# v0.8.0 — The simulator plays a 360 video from your own device on the sphere

Steve asked for local simulations from 360 camera footage. The simulator already played a dataset folder from the
visitor's computer; it now plays a 360 video the same way, without a playlist, so anyone can see their footage on a
sphere before anything is made from it.

## What it decides

**"Open a 360 video…" plays one video file on the sphere.**
- **Where:** under "Open a dataset folder…" on the simulator's panel. Dropping a single video onto the sphere does
  the same.
- **What it takes:** the stitched 360 video a camera's app exports: MP4, MOV, M4V or WebM, with a 2:1 frame.
- **Local only:** the browser reads the file and plays it from memory. Nothing is uploaded, and nothing is counted.
- **As recorded:** at its own speed, with no holds, no tilt and Spin off. Dragging and the arrow keys turn it, as with
  any dataset.
- **The panel** shows its file name, its size and "as recorded". It says the video isn't uploaded, and how a scene
  looks from outside a sphere.

**It reads correctly from outside, so there's no mirror control.** An equirectangular 360 frame maps onto the sphere
the way a map of the Earth does, so text in the scene reads left to right from outside. That was checked on a frame
labelled FRONT, LEFT and RIGHT. What changes is the view: the sky fills the top, the ground the bottom, and the
camera's mount sits at the bottom pole.

**It says when something's wrong, instead of showing a blank sphere.**
- **Not 2:1:** "not the 2:1 frame of a stitched 360 video", with the fix: export the 360 (equirectangular) version.
- **Too wide for this device's graphics** (WebGL's largest texture): the limit and a size to export at.
- **Can't decode it:** export H.264 from the camera's app. When this browser can't decode H.265, the message names it,
  since 360 cameras often record in it.
- A dataset folder keeps its own MPEG-4 Part 2 message.

## For visitors

- Simulator: open a 360 video from your camera and see it on the sphere. It plays on your device and isn't uploaded.

## What it does not decide

- **Making a dataset from a 360 video.** A recipe that scales a 360 video to 4096×2048 and encodes it with the
  validated H.264 encoder, ready for a sphere, is the next step (v0.8.1).
- **Raw camera files.** The two fisheye pictures a camera records need its own app to stitch them first.
- **Sound.** The simulator plays every video muted, as before.

## Tested

- **`site/js/video360.test.mjs`:** which files open as a video, the title from the file name, the frame checks (2:1,
  and the graphics limit with the size to export at), and the can't-play words with and without H.265.
- **`site/js/playlist.test.mjs`:** a folder without a playlist finds a `.mov`.
- **`tests/test_browser.py::test_simulator_plays_a_360_video`,** in Chrome with real decoding:
  - a red 2:1 video fills the panel with its name and size, clears the gallery's dataset and address, shows red on
    the sphere and warns of nothing;
  - a 16:9 video warns that it isn't 2:1;
  - a file that isn't a video says to export H.264;
  - no request reaches the counting API, and the page records no problems.
- **By hand:** the FRONT, LEFT and RIGHT frame, screenshotted on the sphere. Dropping a single video isn't covered
  by the browser test.
- **The suite:** 179 Python tests (with the browser tests required) and 27 node tests pass. `infra/` is unchanged,
  and its 23 tests pass.
