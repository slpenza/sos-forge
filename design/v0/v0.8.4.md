# v0.8.4 — A 360 video becomes a dataset for a real sphere

v0.8.0 let the simulator play a 360 video from your device. This version makes one into a dataset: the same movie
files, checks and playlist as any sos-forge movie, ready to copy onto a sphere.

## What it decides

**A `video` layer paints each movie frame from a 360 video.**
- **What it takes:** the stitched 2:1 video a 360 camera's app exports (MP4, MOV, M4V, WebM or MKV), as `path`: a
  file, or a pattern such as `data/*` that matches exactly one. None, or several, is refused by name.
- **A 360 frame or nothing:** a video that isn't 2:1, within 2%, is refused before anything is encoded, with what to
  export instead. The build's existing seam check then confirms that frame 0 wraps at ±180°, as a stitched video does.
- **The whole clip spans the movie:** frame i shows the clip at i × length ÷ frames. With `[animation] frames = 0`
  the movie is as long as the clip, at its recorded speed. `sos build --seconds N` fits the whole clip into N
  seconds, as a time-lapse or slow motion.
- **Decoding:** ffmpeg decodes at the movie's size and frame times. Each worker keeps one decoder reading forward,
  and starts it again with a seek when it's asked to go back, or more than 90 frames ahead. Past the clip's last
  frame, it holds that frame.
- **`credit`** names the footage's maker in the playlist and README.

**`[animation] frames = 0` asks a layer for the movie's length.** A layer that can say, as `video` does, gives it; a
recipe with none is refused, so `0` can't build an empty movie.

**`my_360_video` is the ready-made recipe.**
- **How:** copy one video into `recipes/my_360_video/data/` and run `sos build my_360_video`.
- **Private:** the folder is gitignored except its README, so footage never reaches the repository.
- **Text:** the title goes on as SOS labels (`[hud] mode = "labels"`), so nothing is painted over the footage.
- **Shape:** 4096 wide, 30 fps, as long as the clip, at its own speed.

## For visitors

- sos-forge: the my_360_video recipe makes your 360 camera's video into a dataset for a sphere.

## What it does not decide

- **Sound.** SOS movies are silent, so the video's sound is dropped.
- **Raw camera files.** Two fisheye pictures need the camera's app to stitch them first.
- **Levelling or turning the scene.** The footage is used as the camera's app exported it.
- **Publishing your footage to the gallery.** A dataset you make stays yours.

## Tested

- **`tests/test_video.py`,** against real ffmpeg:
  - `probe` reads the size, codec and length, and refuses a file that isn't a video;
  - `find_video` finds one video by pattern or name, skips other files, and refuses none or two, naming them;
  - the reader follows the clip: forward, back again, far ahead, and past its end;
  - an end-to-end build of a 2-second clip:
    - `frames = 0` gives 20 frames at 10 fps, rendered by two workers;
    - the clip's colours land at the right times;
    - the title is a label on every frame, the credit is in the README, and every check passes;
    - `--seconds 1` fits the whole clip into 10 frames;
  - a 16:9 video is refused before anything is encoded;
  - `frames = 0` with no video, and a video layer without a path, are refused.
- **By hand, at full size:** `sos build my_360_video` on a 5-second 3840×1920 clip.
  - 150 frames at 4096×2048 in MPEG-4 and H.264, and every check passed, in 72 seconds;
  - the output matched the source at frames 0, 75 and 149, to a mean difference of under 2 levels in 255;
  - a clip that doesn't wrap at ±180° (ffmpeg's flat test pattern) failed the seam check, as it should.
- **The suite:** 195 Python tests (with the browser tests required), 27 node tests and 26 infra tests pass.
