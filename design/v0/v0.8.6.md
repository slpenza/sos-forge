# v0.8.6 — Where 8 Billion People Live: how many people live in each part of the world

A still image for the gallery, chosen by Steve from four candidates: where the world's 8.2 billion people live in 2025,
as people per square kilometre on a log scale. It shows what a map of night lights can't: places without electric
light hold people too.

## What it decides

**The data is GHSL's population grid, GHS-POP R2023A, for 2025.**
- **What it is:** the European Commission Joint Research Centre's grid of residential population, people per
  30-arcsecond cell, from census data disaggregated by built-up area. The 2025 values are estimates projected from
  those censuses. It totals 8.192 billion.
- **Terms:** CC BY 4.0, © European Union, as GHSL's Data Package 2023 (in the download) and its site state. The
  dataset carries GHSL's citation with its DOI, 10.2905/2FF68A52-5B5B-4A22-8F40-C41DA8332CFE, and
  `docs/data-sources.md` lists it.
- **The download:** 484 MB, once, checked against a fixed size and SHA-256 like Cities Growing's, then kept in the
  cache. So is the extracted GeoTIFF.

**A `ghsl_pop` source sums the grid into cells the image can use.**
- **How:** it reads the 43,202 × 21,384 GeoTIFF tile by tile and gives each source cell to the coarse cell holding
  its centre. The coarse cells are 1/12° by default, about 9 km at the equator, near the image's own resolution.
  Read whole, the grid is 7.4 GB; tile by tile, it takes about 15 seconds.
- **What it drops:** the grid's extra column beyond ±180° on each side, which would count twice, and missing,
  negative or NaN cells, which count as nobody.
- **What it gives:** people per km² at the cell centres, and the total as `people` and `people_billions` for the
  title. The summed cells are cached, so a rebuild skips the sum.

**The grid layer gains a log scale and a legend.**
- **`scale = "log"`:** each tenfold rise climbs the colour ramp by the same step. Values under `min` stay dark.
- **`legend = true`:** draws the same colour bar Getting Richer has, at the HUD's anchors. Its drawing moved from the
  regions layer into `sosforge/legend.py`, so both layers share it, and Getting Richer is unchanged.
- **Stats:** a source's named numbers are HUD values, so the subtitle reads "8.2 billion people, 2025" from the data
  rather than a typed number.

**The recipe, `where_people_live`.**
- **The image:** people per km² from 1 to 10,000 on the `fire` ramp, glowing over a dimmed Blue Marble. Blue Marble
  is daytime imagery, not night lights, so it doesn't tell the same story as the data.
- **Shape:** 4096 × 2048, with the title and the legend at the usual three ocean anchors.
- **In the gallery:** a still image, one 1.9 MB zip on the `data-2026-11` release, with its source linked to the
  DOI.

## What it does not decide

- **Other years.** GHSL has 1975 to 2030 in five-year steps; a movie of them is a later dataset.
- **Night lights beside it.** Comparing the two would be a dataset of its own.

## Tested

- **`tests/test_v3_sources.py::test_ghsl_pop_sums_people`,** on a small GeoTIFF with GHSL's georeferencing, written
  in 16-pixel tiles:
  - people land in the cell holding their centre;
  - the columns beyond ±180°, and missing, negative and NaN cells, count as nobody;
  - the cell areas add up to the sphere's;
  - density is people per km² at cell centres, with the total reported;
  - a bad `cells_per_degree` is refused before any download.
- **`tests/test_v3_layers.py::test_grid_log_scale_and_legend`:**
  - each tenfold rise brightens by the same step, and values under the minimum stay dark;
  - the source's numbers reach the HUD;
  - the legend is drawn at the HUD's anchor;
  - an unknown scale, and a log scale with a minimum of 0, are refused.
- **Getting Richer's legend tests** pass unchanged on the shared legend code.
- **By hand, the real data:**
  - the 2025 grid sums to 8.192 billion;
  - the densest cell holds 67,448 people per km²;
  - the cell areas match the Earth's surface to one part in a million.
- **By hand, the image:**
  - every check passed, including the seam at ±180°;
  - looked at in full and at full size over South and East Asia;
  - the background was dimmed once, so Greenland and Antarctica stop competing.
- **The suite:** 198 Python tests (with the browser tests required), 27 node tests and 26 infra tests pass.
- **Not tested before release:** the gallery card, which needs the zip on the release first.
