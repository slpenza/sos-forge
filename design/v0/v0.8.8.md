# v0.8.8 — Fires Burning Now: every fire seen from orbit in the last 24 hours

A still image for the gallery, chosen by Steve: every fire NASA's VIIRS instruments detected in the 24 hours before
the build, glowing by the heat each gave off. Like Tonight's Aurora, it carries its date in its name, so a build stays
true after the day it shows.

## What it decides

**The data is NASA FIRMS's open active-fire files.**
- **What it is:** VIIRS 375 m detections from NOAA-20 and NOAA-21. Suomi NPP stopped delivering on 2 November 2026,
  and MODIS on Terra ends on 1 December, so these are the two still flying.
- **Where from:** FIRMS's global CSV files, which it refreshes about every hour. sos-forge fetches them with no key
  and caches them for an hour.
- **Terms:** NASA data, which NASA shares fully and openly. FIRMS asks to be acknowledged with its name, NASA's ESDIS
  and a link, and the dataset's credits carry all three. `docs/data-sources.md` lists it.

**A `firms` source reads them, and keeps only the period it's named for.**
- **What it reads:** each satellite's file, keeping detections of at least nominal confidence. VIIRS marks
  low-confidence detections, for example sun glint.
- **The window:** the "24h" files reach further back than their name; the first fetch held 43 hours. The source keeps
  the 24 hours before the latest detection: 87,248 of 169,476. So the subtitle's "24 hours to …" is true.
- **What it gives:** points with fire radiative power in megawatts, plus `fires_from` and `fires_to`, the window's
  start and end.
- **Its limits:** an empty file, such as a satellite that's down, is skipped. Files that are all empty are refused,
  rather than making a blank image.

**Points layers hand their source's times to the HUD,** as grid layers already did, so the recipe's name and subtitle
take the date and time from the data.

**The recipe, `fires_burning_now`.**
- **The image:** each detection glows orange-red, sized by the square root of its fire radiative power, over a dimmed
  Blue Marble. Blue Marble is daytime imagery, so city lights don't pass for fires.
- **The words:** it counts "detections", not fires. A big fire covers many pixels, and both satellites see it.
- **The text:** the subtitle reads, for example, "87,248 detections in the 24 hours to 25 Nov 19:23 UTC". Its panel
  is widened to 50° so the line stays readable.
- **Known limits:** a few detections are gas flares and industrial heat, which satellites see the same way, and the
  description says so.
- **In the gallery:** a still image, one 1.3 MB zip on the `data-2026-11` release, named "Fires Burning Now, 25 Nov
  2026". It's linked to FIRMS, with its window as the card's date.

## For visitors

- New dataset: Fires Burning Now, every fire seen from orbit in a day, named with its date.

## What it does not decide

- **Refreshing it.** Like the aurora, the published image is one day's. A newer one is a rebuild and a new zip.
- **The LA fires of January 2025.** They need FIRMS's archive, which takes a key, and a zoomed inset; Steve chose
  this first.
- **Separating industrial heat from fires.** FIRMS's open files don't mark which is which.

## Tested

- **`tests/test_v3_sources.py::test_firms_reads_fires`,** with stubbed downloads:
  - both satellites' files are fetched once, then read from the cache;
  - low-confidence detections, and those older than 24 hours before the latest, are left out, and `fires_from` and
    `fires_to` are that window;
  - fire radiative power is kept;
  - unknown satellites, periods and confidences, an unreadable row, and files with no detections are refused.
- **`tests/test_v3_layers.py::test_points_pass_source_times`:** a points layer's source's times reach the HUD.
- **By hand, the live files:**
  - 82,653 and 86,823 detections, cut to 87,248 in the 24 hours to 25 Nov 19:23 UTC;
  - fire radiative power has a median of 5 MW and a maximum of 813 MW.
- **By hand, the image:**
  - every check passed, including the seam at ±180°;
  - looked at in full, over Africa at full size, and at the panel's text, which was shortened and widened once;
  - the credits carry FIRMS's link.
- **The suite:** 200 Python tests (with the browser tests required), 27 node tests and 26 infra tests pass.
- **Not tested before release:** the gallery card, which needs the zip on the release first.
