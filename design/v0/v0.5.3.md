# v0.5.3 — Text a step larger, nothing under 14 px, and Help without its heading

Steve asked whether older visitors would struggle to read the site, and said Help looked condensed. An audit of the
live pages found text as small as 10.5 px on Help's front page (the topic groups' names), 11.5 px (file names),
11.8 px (the Ask buttons) and 12 px (What's new's tags). The faint grey for dates, sources and file names measured
4.1 to 4.7:1 against its backgrounds, under the 4.5:1 WCAG asks of small text on some surfaces. On a phone, Help's
side gutter was 18 px against the home page's 12 px, so its question box was narrower. Its heading, "Ask about
sos-forge", repeated what the bar already says.

## What it decides

**The site reads at 17 px, and no text is smaller than 14 px.**
- **The base:** body text goes from 16 px to 17 px, with a line height of 1.6.
- **The floor:** every size that was under .85rem is now .85rem, about 14.5 px:
  - the card's Data line and catalog line;
  - badges;
  - the simulator's panel headings;
  - What's new's kind tags.
- **Code:** at least 14 px.
- **The home page's ask box:** its text grows with the base (`--ask-size` 15 to 17 px), and its Ask button is held
  to 14 px.
- **Turned down:** 18 px or more. The cards and the simulator's panel would wrap far more, for a gain the floor
  already gives.

**The faint grey is lighter: `#747d8e` becomes `#8a93a3`.**
- **Its contrast:** at least 5.5:1 on the page, the cards and the fields, against 4.1 to 4.7 before.
- **Help:** gets the same colour through `--doc-faint` and `--ask-dim`.

**Help scales the same way, from the head fragment sos-forge already owns.** `site/help-head.html` goes into every
help page's head. It already set Help's colours, so it can set its sizes too.
- **The base:** `--doc-size` and `--ask-size` go to 17 px, and `--doc-leading` to 1.7. Every Help size is a ratio
  of those.
- **The topic list:**
  - titles at 17 px;
  - summaries at 15.5 px with a 1.55 line height;
  - group names and file names at 14 px.
- **Elsewhere on Help:** code is at least 14 px, and so are the Ask button and the loading line.
- **Its side gutter** is the site's, `clamp(12px, 2.2vw, 24px)`. On a phone the question box is as wide as the
  home page's.
- **"Ask about sos-forge" leaves the screen.** It stays in the page, visually hidden, as the panel's heading for
  screen readers. Leaving it out of the frame would only bring back Ask & Answer's default, "Ask questions and get
  help".
- **A correction:** v0.4.10's request for Ask & Answer to drop the panel's frame said sos-forge couldn't restyle
  Help. It could, through this fragment. The change Ask & Answer made also serves the other desk site, so it stands.

## For visitors

- Gallery: text across the site is a step larger and higher in contrast, with nothing smaller than 14 px.
- Help: larger, roomier text, no heading over the question box, and on a phone the box is as wide as the home
  page's.

## What it does not decide

- **The simulator's sphere.** Its labels are drawn at the dataset's own size; that is SOS's, not the page's.
- **Text that isn't the site's:** dataset previews and what a dataset's panels paint into the picture.

## Tested

- **An audit of every page, at 1440 px and 390 px:** the smallest text and the lowest small-text contrast, on the
  live site and on a local build with Help:
  - **before (live):** 10.5 px on Help's front page, 11.8 px on topic pages, 11.9 px on the home page, 12 px on What's
    new and 12.2 px in the simulator. Lowest contrast 4.40.
  - **after (local build):** 14.0 px on every page, the 404 page at 17 px. Lowest small-text contrast 5.89.
  - no page errors and no sideways scroll.
- **The simulator's Pause button** read 1.52 in that audit. That is the script taking its translucent amber wash as
  solid, not the button: it is amber on near-black.
- **New browser tests:**
  - `test_no_text_is_smaller_than_14px` on the home page, the simulator, What's new and the 404 page;
  - the Help test now checks the help landing has no text under 14 px and that its heading is only for screen
    readers.
- **The suite:** 169 Python tests and 18 node tests pass, with the browser tests required.
