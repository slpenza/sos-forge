# v0.5.2 — Dragging the simulator turns the picture, so SOS labels visibly stay put

Steve reported that the labels "fixed on the sphere" moved with the sphere. A drag walked the viewpoint around the
sphere, so the picture and the labels turned together on screen, as they would for someone walking round a real
one. The one thing that sets SOS labels apart, that they stay put while the picture turns, only showed under the
Spin button.

## What it decides

**A sideways drag turns the picture about its poles, as a presenter does from the iPad.**
- **What turns:** the picture, not the viewpoint. It adds to the angle Spin advances, eastward for a rightward
  drag.
- **What stays:** SOS labels are drawn in the room's frame, so they stay where they are while the picture moves
  under them. Painted panels turn with it.
- **The arrow keys:** ← and → turn it 5° at a time, as they used to move the viewpoint.
- **Unchanged:** an up-and-down drag still looks from higher or lower, wheel and pinch still step closer, and Orbit
  and Visitor still set the viewpoint.
- **What goes:** walking around to see the other three label copies. They are the same line, and the one in front
  is what a visitor reads.
- **Turned down:** keeping the walk-around and adding a "turn" mode. A presenter's one gesture is turning the
  globe, and a second mode would be one more thing to find.
- **Other callers:** `attachControls` still orbits the view when it isn't given a turn callback.

## For visitors

- Simulator: dragging sideways turns the picture, as a presenter does, and SOS labels stay put while it moves.

## What it does not decide

- **Keeping a turned picture across a version switch.** A switch starts the picture facing front again, as before.

## Tested

- **`test_browser.py::test_simulator_labels_on_the_sphere`,** comparing the sphere drawn with labels on and off,
  while paused:
  - Spin, a 60 px sideways drag and three → presses each turn the picture, and the label's centre doesn't move;
  - a 40 px downward drag moves the label more than 10 px, since the whole view moves;
  - three runs out of three pass.
- **The suite:** 165 Python tests and 18 node tests pass, with the browser tests required.
- **Not tested:** touch. A one-finger drag takes the same path as a mouse drag, and pinch is unchanged.
