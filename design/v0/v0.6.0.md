# v0.6.0 — Suggest a dataset: a link, its licence and what it would show, kept with the rest of the correspondence

Visitors who know of open data that would make a good sphere dataset have no way to say so, short of finding an
email address. A form on the site takes the one thing needed to judge a suggestion: where the data is, under what
licence, and what it would show.

## What it decides

**`suggest.html`, "Suggest a dataset".**
- **Where it's linked:** as "Suggest" in every page's nav (Help's bar too), and from a line under the home page's
  introduction.
- **Fields:**
  - **Where the data is:** a link, required, `http` or `https`, at most 500 characters.
  - **Its licence:** a choice, required. CC0 1.0, CC BY 4.0, CC BY-SA 4.0, ODbL 1.0, Public domain (a US Government
    work), Other, or I don't know. "Other" asks for a link to the licence.
  - **What it would show on a sphere:** required, at most 1,000 characters.
  - **Your email:** optional, only for a reply.
- **The declaration, a required checkbox:** "To my knowledge the licence above is the one this data is published
  under. SOS Forge checks every licence before using any data." It is a statement of what the visitor believes, not
  a grant of rights or a contract. Wording that grants rights would need someone qualified to write it.
- **The privacy note, beside the button:**
  - what is kept, where, and for how long;
  - the email is used only to reply;
  - nothing is published, and nothing is shared beyond the maintainer.
- **After sending:** a thank-you that says suggestions are read, and that not every one can be built.

**A submission becomes an email to sos@stevenpenza.com, so it is kept and seen where NOAA's correspondence is.**
- **The route:** `POST /api/suggest` on sos.stevenpenza.com itself. CloudFront sends that path to an API Gateway
  HTTP API, so the CSP stays `connect-src 'self'` and no new origin is allowed.
- **The Lambda:**
  - validates every field again, server-side;
  - sends one plain-text email through SES, From `SOS Forge suggestions <sos-suggest@stevenpenza.com>` to
    `sos@stevenpenza.com`;
  - marks it with `X-SOS-Forge-Suggestion: 1`, and sets Reply-To to the visitor's email only when one is given.
- **What happens to it:** stevenpenza.com's receipt rule stores it under `mail/sos/`. Steve's arrival notice fires,
  because it isn't from sos@. The local `sos admin` page lists it under a new **Suggestions** section.
- **How the admin page files it:** by that header before any dataset-name match, so a suggestion that names a
  dataset isn't filed as NOAA correspondence.
- **Permissions:**
  - SES only from `sos-suggest@stevenpenza.com` and only to `sos@stevenpenza.com`, as IAM conditions on
    `ses:FromAddress` and `ses:Recipients`, on the stevenpenza.com identity;
  - `dynamodb:UpdateItem` on its one table, and reading its one secret. Nothing else.
- **The checks:** `site/js/suggest.js` checks the same fields in the same order, in the server's words. A test holds
  the two to the same licence list and messages.

**Bots are turned away by a honeypot and a minimum fill time, and floods by rate limits.**
- **The honeypot:** a `website` field hidden from people and from screen readers, kept out of the tab order and
  autocomplete. Filled in, the request gets the same thank-you, and nothing is sent.
- **The fill time:** the page records when the form was shown. A submission under 3 seconds later gets the same
  silent thank-you.
- **The rate limits:**
  - **per visitor:** at most 3 suggestions an hour from one IP address, counted in a DynamoDB table.
    - **How the address is kept:** only as an HMAC-SHA256 under a secret salt, and each row expires after 24 hours
      (TTL).
    - **Where it comes from:** CloudFront's `CloudFront-Viewer-Address` header.
  - **for everyone:** at most 25 a day in total, so a flood can't fill sos@ or run up SES. Past either limit the
    visitor is told to try again later (429), and nothing is sent;
  - **at the API:** API Gateway throttling of 1 request a second, with bursts of 5, before the Lambda runs.
- **Turned down:** a CAPTCHA service. It means a third party on every visit, and a honeypot with limits fits the
  likely volume.
- **Turned down:** AWS WAF. A rate-based rule needs a web ACL that costs every month, for a form that may see a few
  suggestions a week.

## What it does not decide

- **Calls that skip CloudFront.** The API's own execute-api address still answers, and a caller there could forge the
  viewer header to dodge the per-visitor limit. The daily limit and the API's throttling still hold, and a
  suggestion still lands only in the mailbox. Closing it means a custom domain or a header secret CloudFront adds.
- **Replying from the admin page.** It sends only to NOAA. Replying to a suggester is Steve's own mail, using the
  email in the suggestion.
- **A QR code for museum visitors.** The form's address works as a QR target; a printed code, and whether it
  carries the site it came from, is a later version.
- **Building what is suggested.** A suggestion is a lead, judged by hand; nothing is queued or promised.
- **Deleting old suggestions.** They are kept like the rest of `mail/sos/` (versioned, no expiry) until Steve
  removes them. The privacy note says so.

## Before building

- **The deploy role's reach, checked read-only on 24 Nov 2026:**
  - this is the first version whose stack has a Lambda, an HTTP API, a DynamoDB table and an IAM role;
  - `sosforge-deploy-role` may only assume `cdk-sos1-*` roles, and the stack deploys through the `sos1`
    bootstrap (`infra/cdk.json`);
  - `cdk-sos1-cfn-exec-role` has AdministratorAccess, so CloudFormation can create them with no change to the
    deploy role.
- **The salt:** a random 48-character value in Secrets Manager, generated by the stack, never in the repo or the
  template, and read once per Lambda container. CloudFormation can't create an SSM SecureString, which the scope
  first named. The secret costs about $0.40 a month.

## Tested

- **The Lambda, `infra/test/suggest.test.mjs`, 7 tests with fake ports:**
  - every field's validation, including header injection in the email;
  - the visitor's address from CloudFront's header, IPv4 and IPv6;
  - the email: From, To, Reply-To only when an email was given, the suggestion mark, and a base64 body in lines of
    at most 76 characters;
  - the honeypot, a fast fill, a missing or future stamp each give a silent 200, and nothing is sent or counted;
  - wrong method, wrong type, bad JSON and a bad field are refused without sending;
  - 3 an hour per visitor and the daily limit give a 429, and the table's key never holds the address;
  - a failed send gives a 502.
- **The stack, `infra/test/site-stack.test.mjs`:**
  - `/api/*` is HTTPS-only, uncached, and passes only Content-Type and CloudFront-Viewer-Address;
  - one route, `POST /api/suggest`, on a stage throttled to 1 a second with bursts of 5;
  - the table has TTL, and the Lambda runs Node 24 with the limits in its environment;
  - its policy:
    - SES only from the suggestions address and only to the mailbox;
    - only `dynamodb:UpdateItem`, and its secret;
    - no wildcard actions anywhere in the stack;
  - the CSP still allows only the site and Ask & Answer.
- **The form, `site/js/suggest.test.mjs`:** web addresses, and every check matching the server's field and words,
  with the same licence list.
- **The page, `test_browser.py::test_suggest_form`,** in headless Chrome under the deployed CSP, with the API
